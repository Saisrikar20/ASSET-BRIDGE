# Bank deposits (RBI)
## Claiming as a nominee
When a depositor dies and a valid nominee is registered, the bank pays the nominee on receipt of the bank's claim form, a death certificate, and the nominee's KYC (PAN and Aadhaar or other officially valid document). The nominee receives the money as a trustee for the legal heirs; the nomination does not override the deceased's will or succession law. Banks follow RBI instructions to settle deceased-depositor claims within a fixed number of days of receiving complete documents.
## Claiming without a nominee
If there is no nominee, or the nominee has also died, the legal heirs claim together. Banks generally ask for a death certificate, a legal heir certificate (or succession certificate for larger amounts), a letter of indemnity signed by all heirs, and sometimes a surety. Up to a bank-specific threshold, banks accept an indemnity instead of a court-issued succession certificate. Above that threshold, a succession certificate from a civil court under the Indian Succession Act, 1925 is usually required.
## Joint accounts
For a joint account with "either or survivor" or "former or survivor" mandate, the surviving holder gets the balance on producing the death certificate; no heir certificate is needed.
## Unclaimed deposits and the DEA Fund
Deposits that have not been operated for 10 years are transferred by banks to the RBI's Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund. The money is not lost: the depositor or legal heirs can still claim it, with interest where applicable, through the same bank. The bank pays and then seeks reimbursement from the DEA Fund.
## RBI UDGAM portal
UDGAM (Unclaimed Deposits – Gateway to Access inforMation) is an RBI portal where anyone can search unclaimed deposits across participating banks using the account holder's name and one of PAN, voter ID, driving licence, passport, or date of birth. It shows which bank holds the deposit; the claim itself is filed at that bank.
## Inoperative accounts
A savings or current account with no customer-induced transaction for two years becomes inoperative. It can be re-activated by updating KYC at any branch or via video KYC where offered; no charge is levied for re-activation.
