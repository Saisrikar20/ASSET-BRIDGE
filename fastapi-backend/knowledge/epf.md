# Provident fund and pension (EPFO)
## Form 20 – PF balance on death
Form 20 is used by the nominee, or legal heirs if there is no nominee, to claim the deceased member's provident fund balance. It needs the death certificate, bank details with a cancelled cheque, and identity proof. If no nomination exists, a legal heir certificate or succession certificate is attached. The form can be filed online on the EPFO Unified Portal by a nominee whose Aadhaar is seeded, or physically through the employer or the EPFO regional office.
## Form 10D – family pension under EPS-95
Form 10D is filed to start monthly family pension under the Employees' Pension Scheme, 1995. The surviving spouse gets pension first; children up to age 25 get children's pension (up to two children at a time); orphan pension applies if both parents have died. Documents: death certificate, bank details, photographs, and age proof for children.
## Form 5(IF) – EDLI insurance
The Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance scheme pays a lump-sum insurance benefit to the nominee or legal heirs of a member who dies while in service. Claim through Form 5(IF), attested by the employer, with the death certificate. The benefit has a statutory minimum and maximum set by EPFO.
## Multiple UANs and inoperative accounts
Members who changed jobs often end up with more than one UAN or old member IDs that were never transferred. Old balances should be merged into the active UAN with Form 13 (transfer claim) or by reporting the duplicate UAN to EPFO. An EPF account that receives no contribution for 36 months after the member stops working is classified as inoperative; it still earns interest until the member turns 58, and the balance remains claimable.
## Without nomination
EPFO pays legal heirs in equal shares according to the family definition under the EPF Scheme, 1952 when no nomination exists. Updating e-nomination on the Unified Portal (with Aadhaar of nominees) avoids this delay.
