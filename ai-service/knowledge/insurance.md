# Life insurance (IRDAI and LIC)
## Death claim with a valid nominee
The nominee informs the insurer of the death (online, call centre, or branch) and submits the claim form, the death certificate, the original policy document, nominee KYC, and a bank mandate for NEFT. For deaths in the first three years of the policy, insurers may investigate before paying. Under IRDAI rules, insurers must settle or repudiate a death claim within a fixed time after receiving all documents, and pay interest for delays.
## When the nominee is missing or has died
The insurer pays the legal heirs. It will ask for proof of title: a legal heir certificate, a succession certificate, or a probated will, together with an indemnity from the heirs. Registering a new nominee while the policyholder is alive is the simplest fix — it is a free endorsement request.
## Minor nominee
If the nominee is a minor, the policyholder should name an appointee who receives the money on the minor's behalf. Without an appointee, the insurer may require a guardianship certificate from court.
## LIC claims
LIC death claims are filed at the servicing branch. Typical documents: Claim Form A (claimant's statement), certified death certificate, original policy bond, the claimant's KYC and bank details (NEFT mandate), and, if there is no valid nominee, proof of title such as a legal heir or succession certificate. Matured policies whose amounts were never collected show up as unclaimed; LIC publishes a search for unclaimed amounts on its website using policy number, name, date of birth and PAN.
## Unclaimed insurance money
Insurers must display unclaimed amounts over a minimum value on their websites, searchable by policyholders and heirs. Amounts unclaimed for more than 10 years are transferred to the Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund; they remain claimable for 25 years after transfer. IRDAI's Bima Bharosa portal handles grievances when an insurer delays or denies a claim.
